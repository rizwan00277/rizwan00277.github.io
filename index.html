I am a Research Associate (Assistant Manager, Technical) at the AI Technology Centre (AITeC), Islamabad, in the Autonomous AI & Decision Support Lab, working under the supervision of Dr. Muhammad Ahmad Kamran.

My research interests are in computer vision and deep learning, particularly the detection of small objects: targets that occupy only a few pixels in an image, such as distant drones or far-away pedestrians, and that remain difficult for current detectors.

In my current work, I study how lightweight changes to YOLO detectors, such as higher-resolution detection heads, attention and downsampling modules, affect the detection of small drones and distant road users. This includes a comparison of the same modules across three YOLO generations (SD-YOLO) and a paired RGB–thermal benchmark built on the FLIR ADAS dataset.

At AITeC, I also apply vision methods on real platforms: camera-based localization of multiple ground robots, deep reinforcement learning for robot navigation, and vision-based flight for an indoor drone. I also help supervise student projects in the lab.

Before joining AITeC, I completed my MS in Computer Science at the Pakistan Institute of Engineering and Applied Sciences (PIEAS) under the supervision of Prof. Asifullah Khan, where my thesis worked on improving YOLOv8 for the detection of small and distant drones. I was awarded the Gold Medal (CGPA 3.8/4.0). I completed my BS in Computer Science at Government College University (GCU), Lahore, and have taught as a Lecturer at the University of Lahore and as a Teaching Assistant at PIEAS.

Research interests: small-object detection · efficient and lightweight detectors · multimodal (RGB–thermal) perception · aerial and drone-based vision.

I am seeking PhD opportunities starting Fall 2027 in computer vision and deep learning, and would be glad to hear from groups working on related problems.

selected publications

  1. Journal
    Cross-Generation Small-Drone Detection with Compact YOLO Modules
    Muhammad Rizwan, Abdul Rehman Khan, Muhammad Muzaffar Khan, and 5 more authors
    2026
    Journal article, under review
    Abs Website

    Lightweight modules for small-drone detection are usually tested on a single YOLO release with frame-level splits, so it is unclear whether they help other YOLO generations. We add a stride-4 (P2) head, followed by attention, downsampling and neck modules chosen stage by stage on validation, to YOLOv8n, YOLO11n and YOLO26n under one protocol, and evaluate all 48 configuration-generation combinations on 46 test videos of a video-disjoint Drone-vs-Bird split. Selected on validation, P2 + ELA + ADown raises the test mAP50-95 of YOLO11n and YOLO26n by 26% and 35% and their small-object AP by 44% and 60% (Holm-corrected p <= 0.037), with 11-12% fewer parameters; exploratory test-selected variants reach up to 48% higher mAP50-95. Module effects depend on the generation, so lightweight modules should be re-validated for each YOLO release.

  2. ICCII 2026
    A Paired RGB-Thermal Benchmark and the MaxSPD-YOLO Detector for Small Road-User Detection
    Muhammad Rizwan, Muhammad Awais Khan, and Muhammad Ahmad Kamran
    2026
    Submitted to the International Conference on Computing, Intelligence and Innovation (ICCII 2026), under review
    Abs

    We construct a paired RGB-thermal benchmark from FLIR ADAS v1.3, annotating 52,857 objects in RGB images across 9,598 paired frames with shared, leakage-checked splits, and evaluate eleven YOLO configurations in both modalities with three seeds each (66 runs). MaxSPD-YOLO11n, which combines higher-resolution detection heads, space-to-depth down-sampling and a MaxViT block, improves test AP50:95 over YOLO11n from 37.9% to 40.3% in RGB and from 28.2% to 29.9% in thermal, and small-object AP from 31.5% to 36.0% (RGB) and 21.0% to 22.8% (thermal), with 43% fewer parameters but more computation (13.8 vs. 6.4 GFLOPs).

  3. ICIC 2026
    Is It PTSD or the Production? Auditing Shortcut Learning in Video-Based PTSD Detection
    Muhammad Rizwan, Muhammad Awais Khan, and Muhammad Ahmad Kamran
    In International Conference on Innovative Computing (ICIC 2026), 2026
    Accepted, to appear
    Abs

    Deep learning models have reported promising results in detecting post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) from interview videos, but high scores do not show that a model has learned patterns related to the condition. We investigate this on PTSD in the Wild, a dataset of 634 public videos labeled through self-disclosure, comparing facial, behavioral, vocal and textual models with probes that never see the person under progressively stricter subject-grouped, source-disjoint and metadata-balanced evaluation. Under subject-grouped evaluation, container metadata alone reaches an AUC of 0.986, and a background probe with the person removed reaches 0.945, comparable to facial models. On a metadata-balanced subset, facial models drop to AUCs of 0.67-0.73, while interviewee-only voice reaches 0.886 and a transcript-only probe 0.923. Source, production and content differences may substantially influence performance on this benchmark.

  4. ICACS 2027
    Synthetic Skeleton Pretraining for Subject-Independent Aerial Gesture Recognition
    Muhammad Rizwan, Muhammad Awais Khan, and Muhammad Ahmad Kamran
    2026
    Submitted to ICACS 2027 (CV&M track), under review
    Abs

    Recognising human gestures from a UAV is hard because people appear small and distant and subject diversity is limited. On the real-air subset of RoCoG-v2 we build a subject-independent benchmark of six keypoint-based classifiers, including AeroPoseFormer, a lightweight spatio-temporal Transformer. Random video-level splits inflate performance by about 11-16 points; synthetic-skeleton pretraining improves every deep model (macro-F1 +0.10 to +0.25), and AeroPoseFormer reaches 0.59 macro-F1 on a held-out subject recorded at 30 m, a distance absent from training.

  5. ICACS 2027
    Marker-Free Infrastructure-Based Localization of Multiple Ground Robots with Two Stereo Depth Cameras
    Muhammad Rizwan, Muhammad Awais Khan, Muhammad Huzaifa Rahim Malik, and 2 more authors
    2026
    Submitted to ICACS 2027 (RAS&DT track), under review
    Abs

    A marker-free system that localizes heterogeneous ground robots in an 8 x 4 m arena using two ZED 2i stereo cameras. Each camera runs a fine-tuned YOLOv8n detector, lifts detections to 3-D with the stereo point cloud and maps them to arena coordinates through a floor-plane calibration; cross-camera detections are fused by gated Hungarian assignment and a Kalman tracker. Projecting each measurement onto the calibrated floor plane removes a height-dependent bias and reduces mean error from 14.4 to 5.8 cm, with both cameras running at about 30 FPS.
