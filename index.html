I am a Research Associate (Assistant Manager, Technical) at the Autonomous AI & Decision Support Lab (AITeC), National Centre of Physics (NCP), Islamabad, where I work under the supervision of Dr. Muhammad Ahmad Kamran.

My research focuses on making autonomous robots see, localize, and act in the real world. I work across the full stack — from deep learning models to the hardware they run on:

  • Robot learning: deep reinforcement learning (Soft Actor-Critic) for mapless navigation and obstacle avoidance, with sim-to-real transfer from Gazebo to real TurtleBot 4 robots.
  • Perception and localization: multi-camera stereo tracking of multiple robots in a shared arena, visual SLAM for GPS-denied indoor drone flight, and small-object detection with custom YOLO architectures.
  • Edge AI: deploying vision and navigation pipelines on NVIDIA Jetson hardware with ROS 2.

I received my MS in Computer Science from the Pakistan Institute of Engineering and Applied Sciences (PIEAS) as a Gold Medalist (CGPA 3.8/4.0), where my thesis improved the YOLOv8 architecture for detecting small and distant drones, supervised by Dr. Asifullah Khan. Before that, I completed my BS in Computer Science at Government College University (GCU), Lahore. I have also taught as a Lecturer at the University of Lahore and as a Teaching Assistant at PIEAS.

I am applying for PhD positions starting Fall 2027 in robotics, robot learning, and computer vision. Feel free to reach out by email.

selected publications

  1. In Prep.
    Real-Time Multi-Robot Tracking and Global Localization Using Dual Stereo Cameras
    Muhammad Rizwan
    2026
    Manuscript in preparation
    Abs

    We present a multi-camera system that localizes and tracks multiple TurtleBot 4 robots in a shared 8 x 4 m arena. Two fixed ZED 2i stereo cameras are calibrated into a common world frame; YOLO detections are lifted to 3D using stereo depth and fused across cameras with Hungarian assignment, giving each robot a stable identity and a global (x, y) position in real time.

  2. In Prep.
    RSC-YOLOv26n: A Lightweight Detector for Small Drone Detection
    Muhammad Rizwan and Abdul Rehman
    2026

    Abs

  3. MS Thesis
    Improving YOLOv8 for the Detection of Small and Distant Drones
    Muhammad Rizwan
    Pakistan Institute of Engineering and Applied Sciences (PIEAS), Islamabad, 2024
    Supervised by Dr. Asifullah Khan. Gold Medal.
    Abs

    Small drones occupy only a few pixels in an image, which makes them hard for standard object detectors. This thesis modifies the YOLOv8 architecture to retain fine spatial detail and strengthen small-object features, improving the detection of small and distant drones.
